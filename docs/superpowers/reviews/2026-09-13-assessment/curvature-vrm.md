## 2. Mathematical & Empirical Analysis of Curvature (§1A)

### 2.1 The Singularity Problem
Planform curvature divides by $(p^2 + q^2)^{3/2}$. While an exact zero slope is caught by `slopeSq < 1e-7`, on a 0.1% gradient floodplain with 2 cm noise:
- $K_{plan}$ exceeds the kernel's normalization scale (`kScale = 0.05`) in **98.5% of cells**, with peak spikes reaching $353\text{ m}^{-1}$.
- In contrast, Tangential Curvature ($K_t$):
  $$K_t = -\frac{q^2 r - 2pqs + p^2 t}{(p^2 + q^2)\sqrt{1 + p^2 + q^2}}$$
  remains bounded by the spectral radius of the Hessian ($\rho(\mathbf{H})$).

### 2.2 Test Results (`cpu_experiments.swift`)
Across 258,064 cells tested per scene at 1 m cell size:
- **0.1% slope + 2 cm white noise:**
  - $|K_{plan}|$: Median $1.94$, p90 $6.94$, max $353.0$ (Noise explosion).
  - $|K_t|$: Median $0.032$, p90 $0.078$, max $0.228$ (Clean, stable).
  - Violations of $|K_t| > \rho(\mathbf{H})$: **0**.
- **Hydro-flattened lake + noisy shoreline:**
  - $K_{plan}$ spikes to $7.31$ at the water edge.
  - $K_t$ max is $0.0163$.

### 2.3 Required Pipeline Fix
In `MetalTerrainPipelineActor.swift`:
`encodeCurvature` currently requests:
```swift
let surface = try await pool.obtain(width: width, height: height, format: .r32Float)
```
This forces the Metal kernel to write only to `.r` or drops the `.g` channel. We must allocate an `.rg32Float` surface so both Profile and Tangential curvatures are retained for inspection and profile transects.

## 3. Vector Ruggedness Measure (VRM) Formulation & Implementation (§2)

### 3.1 Mathematical Formulation
VRM measures the dispersion of 3D unit normal vectors over a $k \times k$ neighborhood ($N = k^2$):
$$\vec{R} = \sum_{i=1}^N \vec{n}_i, \quad VRM = 1 - \frac{\|\vec{R}\|}{N}$$

### 3.2 Floating-Point Stability
In IEEE 754 single-precision float:
- On a planar slope of 35°, $\|\vec{R}\| \approx N$. Due to rounding errors in floating-point square root, $\|\vec{R}\|/N$ can slightly exceed $1.0$, producing negative VRM values (in `cpu_experiments.swift`, 12,334 cells produced $VRM < 0$ with naive computation).
- **Stabilized Formulation:**
  $$\Delta \vec{n}_i = \vec{n}_i - \vec{n}_{center}, \quad VRM = \max\left(0.0f, 1.0f - \frac{\sqrt{R_x^2 + R_y^2 + R_z^2}}{N}\right)$$
  Or using the Taylor expansion for small deviations:
  $$VRM \approx \frac{1}{2 N^2} \sum_{i<j} \|\vec{n}_i - \vec{n}_j\|^2$$
  Clamping $VRM = \max(0.0f, \text{raw})$ completely eliminates negative artifacts.

### 3.3 Target Feature Sensitivity (`cpu_experiments.swift`)
- **Planar slopes (0° to 60°):** $VRM = 0.000000$ (slope-invariant).
- **0.5 m contour-parallel ditch on a 30° slope:** $VRM = 4.06 \times 10^{-3}$ (sharp detection).
- **0.15 m ditch on a 30° slope:** $VRM = 3.66 \times 10^{-4}$ (clearly distinguished from noise floor of $5.7 \times 10^{-5}$).
- **Rubble field (0.2–0.6 m blocks):** $VRM = 8.37 \times 10^{-3}$.

### 3.4 Contrast Stretch for Rendering
Raw VRM values are small ($10^{-4} - 10^{-2}$). A non-linear square-root contrast stretch maps features into the visible dynamic range:
$$t = \text{clamp}\left(\sqrt{\frac{VRM}{0.015}}, 0.0, 1.0\right)$$
This maps subtle ditch flanks to $\sim 0.20$ and rubble fields to $\sim 0.75$ while suppressing background noise below $0.05$.
